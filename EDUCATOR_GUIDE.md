# Educator Guide

## At a glance

- **Suggested age:** around 6 years (adult may adapt the language and pacing).
- **Time:** about 8–12 minutes, with no timer or speed score.
- **Goal:** represent quantities to ten, find missing parts of ten, and use a counter model to reason through addition and subtraction stories.
- **Materials:** the app only; optional household objects can be used after the lesson to invent another story.

## Lesson path

1. **Count a set.** Learners fill a ten-frame to show 5, leave it empty to show 0, and fill it to show 10. They may tap spaces in any order, clear, check, and retry.
2. **Make ten.** Each round starts with a fixed number of counters. Learners add counters to fill the frame and name the missing part, including `0 + 10`, `5 + 5`, and `7 + 3`.
3. **Little stories.** Six original one-step stories ask learners to add or take away counters, then record the number sentence. A new two-step duck story asks them to add and subtract in sequence.

The learner can choose **Give me a hint** for a strategy reminder or **Show one small step** for a worked first move. These are optional; checking an answer does not require using either hint. Every activity supports a retry and the lesson can be replayed.

## Facilitation and inclusion

Invite the learner to explain how the counters match the story. Treat an incorrect answer as information for another try; the app gives a gentle cue and does not remove points or limit attempts. Adults can read prompts aloud or use the browser's built-in read-aloud button. All controls are buttons with visible keyboard focus, and the ten-frame needs no drag gesture. Layout and controls adapt to narrow screens; reduced-motion preferences are respected.

The example progression begins with a familiar part of ten (five), then revisits the empty and complete sets. If the learner needs concrete objects, pause and make the same quantity with toys, blocks, or fingers. To extend the lesson, ask for another pair that makes ten, or invite the learner to make up a new story whose total stays within ten.

This is a practice activity, not a diagnostic assessment. It does not collect learner identity, demographics, or performance analytics. Progress is saved locally in the current browser so the lesson can resume on that device; it is not uploaded.

## Why this sequence

The Common Core kindergarten operations-and-algebraic-thinking standards describe representing addition and subtraction with objects or drawings, solving within-10 story problems, and decomposing numbers into pairs. The National Centre for Excellence in the Teaching of Mathematics (NCETM) includes number-bond-to-ten work in its number-facts materials. These references support the content choices; they do not validate this specific app as an intervention.
